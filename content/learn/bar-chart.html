1Introducing differences

Bar charts are mostly about comparing. Start by saying there is a difference.

Generally, there is ……
a significant differencebetween A and B
a wide disparityin + noun
There is a significant difference between the figures for men and women. · There is a wide disparity in income between the two countries.

4Comparison and contrast linkers

Transitions (start a new sentence)

Over 90% of exports were fruits; however, industrial exports contributed none.
Almost all female students were interested in art activities. In contrast, the figure for male students was a mere 15%.
Most of the farmland in Europe was damaged by overgrazing. On the other hand, the most significant cause of damage in Asia was wind erosion.

Subordinating conjunctions (join two clauses)

Over 90% of exports were fruits, whereas / while industrial exports contributed none.
Whereas / While over 90% of exports were fruits, industrial exports contributed none.
Although almost all girls were interested in art activities, only 15% of boys took part in them.
After however and in contrast use a comma. Whereas and while join two halves of one sentence.

Sample answer

The bar chart shows the difference between the percentage of females and males in different post-school qualifications in Australia in 1999.

Overall, women had the highest share in two qualifications, while men ranked first in three. The highest figures for women and men were reported in the undergraduate diploma and the skilled vocational diploma, respectively.

There was a significant difference between the proportions of males and females holding a skilled vocational diploma, with the former standing first with 90%. The disparity between the two figures was almost the same for undergraduate and postgraduate diplomas. However, while in the former women had the highest share with 70%, men accounted for the largest percentage in the latter, also with 70%.

Women also ranked first for bachelor's degrees, whereas the share of men was lower by a narrow margin (55% and 45%, respectively). In contrast, for master's degrees the share of females was only two-thirds as high as that of males, as the latter were responsible for the largest share with 60%.

(169 words)
The slide's chart showed the female bar for bachelor's degrees at 50, but the answer says 55%. Each pair adds up to 100% (male 45%), so 55% is correct, and the chart here is drawn at 55. Undergraduate diploma: men 30% (70 + 30 = 100).
