2Three structures for a share

1 · the share / contribution / proportion / percentage of …

The contribution of consumer expenditure in this category was almost the same in three countries, namely Turkey, Spain and Ireland.

2 · X formed / accounted for / was responsible for / comprised y% of …  ·  X contributed y% to …

Money spent on leisure and education in Turkey accounted for just below 4.5% of spending there, which was the largest proportion among the five countries.

3 · The remaining x% …

The remaining 40% was allocated to a variety of other items.
accounted for needs for: “Food accounted for 24%.” Never “accounted 24%”.

3One chart, three body paragraphs

The same information can be written short or long. Compare the three versions and notice what the longer ones add.

Introduction and overview

The pie chart compares the most important reasons for land degradation. Overall, there are three main reasons, among which over-grazing ranks first.

Lean · 27 words

Over-grazing causes the largest share of land degradation with 35%, followed by deforestation (30%). Over-cultivation ranks third with 28%, and the remaining 7% results from other factors.

Fuller · 43 words

Over-grazing is responsible for the most significant share of land degradation with 35%, followed by deforestation (30%). The contribution of over-cultivation is lower than deforestation by a margin of 2%, and the remaining 7% is accounted for by a range of other factors.

Fullest · 55 words

Over-grazing is responsible for the most significant share of land degradation (35%). There is little difference between the figures for deforestation and over-cultivation, as the former contributes the second highest percentage with 30%, while the share of the latter is marginally lower (28%). The remaining 7% is accounted for by a range of other factors.

The longer versions do not add new numbers. They add comparison: “little difference between”, “by a margin of 2%”, “marginally lower”.

5Task: world spending and population

Writing Task 1 · about 20 minutes · at least 150 words

The charts below give information about world spending and population.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Show the sample answer
Sample answer

The pie charts compare the shares of four major items in global expenditure, as well as how the global population is distributed.

Overall, the largest proportion of money in the world is allocated to food, while transport, housing and clothing are other significant items. Moreover, Asia is home to by far the largest share of the world's population.

According to the first chart, the most significant amounts are paid for food (almost a quarter of global expenditure) and transport (almost 20%), while housing also accounts for a considerable proportion (12%). The least among the four is spent on clothing (merely 6%), and the remaining 40% is spent on a variety of other items.

The second chart shows that there is a significant difference between the population of Asia and that of other continents, since approximately three out of every five human beings live in Asia. Europe and the Americas have similar proportions (14% each) and together are home to just over a quarter of the world's population, while the inhabitants of Africa form a mere tenth.

(176 words)
Corrected from the slide: Europe and the Americas together are 14% + 14% = 28%, which is just over a quarter, not “roughly one-third”. Also added “(12%)” for housing and reworded “the population of Asia is responsible for the largest group of humans”.

6Task: spending by a UK school

Writing Task 1 · about 20 minutes · at least 150 words

The three pie charts below show the changes in annual spending by a particular UK school in 1981, 1991 and 2001.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Three charts over time = describe trends (rose, fell, doubled) as well as shares.
Show the sample answer
Sample answer

The charts compare changes in the shares of five different items which comprised the yearly expenses of a British school between 1981 and 2001.

Overall, while the shares of three items rose, there were declines in the remaining two. Moreover, teachers' salaries contributed the largest proportion of expenditure throughout the period, whereas insurance had the smallest share.

Wages paid to the teaching staff accounted for the most significant proportion of expenditure in 1981 (40%) and went up by a quarter, to 50%, before declining slightly to 45%. In comparison, the salaries of non-teaching staff were responsible for just over a quarter of all expenses in 1981 (28%), but their share dipped substantially, by 13 percentage points, over the course of these 20 years.

The share of furniture and equipment was initially as high as that of resources such as books (15%), but while the former dropped by two-thirds before rising more than fourfold to 23%, the latter rose to 20% and then fell by more than half, to just below 10%.

The contribution of insurance rose minimally from 2% to 3% by 1991, before almost tripling to 8% in 2001.

(190 words)
Made more precise than the slide: furniture rose from 5% to 23%, which is “more than fourfold” rather than “fivefold”. “Dipped by 13%” is now “by 13 percentage points”, because the share fell from 28% to 15%. Other workers' 28% is now “just over a quarter”, the closer fraction.
