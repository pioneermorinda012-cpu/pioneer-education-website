3Language for maps that change

When two maps show before and after (or now and planned), you describe changes. Use the passive: the buildings do not change themselves.

ChangePast (map from a year in the past)Future (planned development)
something newwas built · was constructed · was addedwill be built · is to be constructed
something removedwas demolished · was knocked down · was clearedwill be demolished · will disappear
one thing becomes anotherwas replaced by · was converted into · gave way to · made way forwill be replaced by · will be turned into
bigger / smallerwas extended · was expanded · was reduced in sizewill be extended · will be reduced
no changeremained unchanged · stayed the samewill remain unchanged · will stay where it is
Overview for a two-map task: say what the biggest change is, and whether the area became more built-up, more modern or more residential.

4Task: two sites for a supermarket

Writing Task 1 · about 20 minutes · at least 150 words

The map below is of the town of Garlsdon. A new supermarket (S) is planned for the town. The map shows two possible sites for the supermarket.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Garlsdon and the two possible sites, S1 and S2
Redrawn for practice; distances and populations as in the task.
No time change here, so write about location and compare the two sites: who would each one suit, and what are the problems?
Show a model answer
Pioneer model answer

The map shows the town of Garlsdon, which has a population of 65,000, together with two possible sites for a new supermarket, labelled S1 and S2.

Overall, S1 would suit shoppers who drive from the surrounding towns, while S2 would be more convenient for people who live in Garlsdon itself.

S1 is located in the countryside to the north-west of the town, just off the main road to Hindon, which lies 12 kilometres away and has 10,000 inhabitants. As it is outside the built-up area, there would be plenty of space for parking, and it is also close to the railway, which connects Garlsdon with Cransdon, the largest neighbouring town (29,000 people, 25 kilometres to the south-east).

S2, by contrast, is right in the town centre, surrounded by housing and some industry. Local residents could easily walk there, and it can be reached by rail and by the main roads from all three nearby towns, including Bransdon (15,000 people, 16 kilometres to the south-west). However, because the centre is a no-traffic zone, shoppers would not be able to drive to the store or park next to it.

(187 words)

5Task: Islip town centre — now and planned

Writing Task 1 · about 20 minutes · at least 150 words

The maps below show the centre of a small town called Islip as it is now, and plans for its development.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Islip town centre — now
Islip town centre — planned development
Redrawn for practice.

Step 1 · Paraphrase the question

Question: The maps below show the town centre of a small town called Islip as it is now, and plans for its development.

Write your own introduction first, then compare.

Show example introductions
The two maps illustrate the present layout of Islip town centre and the changes that are proposed for it.
The maps compare the centre of Islip today with how it is expected to look after a planned redevelopment.
Change the words (show → illustrate / compare; as it is now → present layout / today; plans for its development → proposed changes / planned redevelopment), but keep the meaning.

Step 2 · Find the changes

Show the list of changes
  • Main road → dual carriageway (ring road) around the centre
  • Old main road → pedestrians-only street with shops
  • Countryside and northern shops → bus station, shopping centre, car park, new housing
  • New housing next to the park; park smaller
  • School and southern housing → no change

Step 3 · Write it

Show a model answer
Pioneer model answer

The maps show the centre of the small town of Islip as it is now, and how it is planned to be redeveloped.

Overall, the plan will turn the town centre into a pedestrian zone surrounded by a ring road, with new transport and shopping facilities and more housing, at the expense of the countryside and part of the park.

At present, a main road runs from east to west through the centre, with shops on both sides. To the north of the shops lies countryside, while the area south of the main road is mostly housing, with a school in the south-west and a park in the south-east.

Under the plan, the main road will be replaced by a dual carriageway that loops around the centre, and the old road will become a pedestrians-only street lined with shops. The countryside to the north will disappear: the northern shops and the land behind them will give way to a bus station, a shopping centre, a car park and new housing. More new housing will be built beside the park, which will be reduced in size. The school and the housing to the south will remain unchanged.

(196 words)

6Quick check

For a planned change, which is best?
Future + passive: will be + past participle.
S1 is ___ the main road to Hindon (very close to it, but not on it).
just off = very close to, beside a road.
Garlsdon ___ a population of 65,000.
A town has a population of …; or “The population of Garlsdon is 65,000.”
Old shops were knocked down and a car park was built in their place. Best word:
A was replaced by B.
Which belongs in the overview?
The overview gives the biggest changes, not single details.
