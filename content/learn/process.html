Process & Cycle Diagrams

How to describe natural and man-made processes: tenses, the passive, tools and methods, and linking stages in order.

1Process or cycle?

Process — has a start and an end: Stage 1 → Stage 2 → Stage 3. Example: making cement.
Cycle — goes round and round, so it has no real end: Stage 1 → 2 → 3 → 1 … End it with: “…and the process / cycle starts over again.”

Processes can be natural (the water cycle, the life cycle of a silkworm) or artificial / man-made (cement production, generating electricity).

Process answers have no numbers to compare. Your overview should say how many stages there are and where the process begins and ends.

2Grammar for processes

A · Tenses

Usually the present simple, for things that happen regularly: The larvae feed on mulberry leaves for up to 6 weeks.
The present perfect can show that one stage is complete: Once the electricity has been generated, it is transmitted to the station.

B · Active or passive?

Natural events — usually active:

The larvae begin forming cocoons around themselves using silk threads.

Passive only to put the focus on the object:

Numerous eggs are laid by a moth and hatch after 10 days.

Artificial (man-made) processes — usually passive, because who does it is not important:

Energy is sent to the substations by means of transmission lines.

If the diagram names the doer, add it with by:

The bricks are dried by a kiln.

C · Describing the tool or method

Avoid writing “by” for everything:

Tools: using · through the use of · by means of — The mixture is cut into individual bricks using a wire cutter.
Ways and methods: through · via · by means of — The oil is transferred to the refinery through a pipeline.

3Linking the stages

The first stage

  • First, / Firstly, / First of all,
  • In the first stage,
  • At / In the beginning,
  • The first stage is when …
  • The process begins / starts / commences when …
  • The process begins with + noun

Middle stages

  • Next, · Then, · Afterwards,
  • After this / that, · Following this / that,
  • In the following stage, · In the stage that follows,
  • During the next / second / third stage,
  • This is followed by + noun
  • When / After / Once <stage A>, <stage B>
  • <stage A>. Once this stage is complete, <stage B>
  • <stage A> before <stage B>
  • <stage A>, after / following which <stage B>
  • <stage A>, which is followed by + noun

The last stage

  • Finally, · Ultimately, · Eventually,
  • The last / final stage is when …
  • The process ends when … / with + noun
  • <stage A> before finally <stage B>

5Task: cement and concrete

Writing Task 1 · about 20 minutes · at least 150 words

The diagrams below show the stages and equipment used in the cement-making process, and how cement is used to produce concrete for building purposes.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Two diagrams: one paragraph each. The second diagram has percentages — use the pie chart language (makes up, accounts for, the remaining).
Show a model answer
Pioneer model answer

The diagrams illustrate how cement is manufactured and how it is then used to produce concrete.

Overall, cement production is an industrial process of five main stages, from raw materials to bagged powder, whereas making concrete is a much simpler procedure in which cement is combined with three other ingredients in a mixer.

To make cement, limestone and clay are first crushed together in a crusher to form a powder. This powder is then combined in a mixer before being passed into a rotating heater, where heat is applied. After heating, the mixture is ground in a grinder, which produces cement, and finally the cement is packed into bags.

In the second process, concrete is made from four materials. The largest component is gravel (small stones), which makes up 50% of the mixture, followed by sand at 25%. Cement accounts for 15%, and the remaining 10% is water. These ingredients are poured into a large rotating concrete mixer, which turns them into concrete.

6Task: hydro-electric power

Writing Task 1 · about 20 minutes · at least 150 words

The diagram below shows how electricity is generated in a hydro-electric power station.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

This diagram mixes a natural part (sun, evaporation, rain: active verbs) with an artificial part (valve, turbine, cables: passive verbs).
Show a model answer
Pioneer model answer

The diagram shows how electricity is generated in a hydro-electric power station and how it reaches consumers.

Overall, the process combines a natural stage, the water cycle that fills a reservoir, with an artificial system in which moving water drives a turbine; the water can then be reused.

At the start, heat from the sun causes water from the sea to evaporate and form clouds. These clouds move inland, where the water falls as rain and collects in a large reservoir behind a dam.

When electricity is needed, a valve in the dam is opened and water flows down to a turbine, which generates electricity. The power is carried by high-voltage cables to a transformer station, from where it is distributed through underground cables to hospitals, schools, industry and homes in the surrounding area. When the valve is closed, the water is pumped back up into the reservoir, so that the cycle can begin again.

(155 words)
