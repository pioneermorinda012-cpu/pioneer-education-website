True / False / Not Given

Nine Pioneer passages, 72 statements, one task type — the one that costs students the most marks. Every answer is proved against a line of the passage, and on a NOT GIVEN the page shows you the sentence that tempted you and explains what it is missing.

How to get these right

  1. FALSE and NOT GIVEN are not the same thing. FALSE means the passage says the opposite. NOT GIVEN means the passage never deals with it at all. Most lost marks are here.
  2. Ask one question: can I point at the line? If you can point at a line that agrees — TRUE. At a line that disagrees — FALSE. If you cannot point at any line, it is NOT GIVEN, however obviously true it feels.
  3. Your own knowledge is not evidence. A statement can be true about the world and still be NOT GIVEN, because the only question is what this passage says.
  4. Hunt the small words. All, every, only, never, always, most, completely — a statement is often FALSE because of one word. The passage says usually; the statement says always.
  5. Comparisons are usually NOT GIVEN. If two things are mentioned but the passage never measures one against the other, a statement comparing them is NOT GIVEN.
  6. So are reasons. The passage says what happened; the statement says why it happened. If the reason is not in the passage, it is NOT GIVEN.
  7. Should is not does. When a writer says something should be done, he is telling you it is not being done — so a statement saying it already happens is not supported.
  8. The statements follow the order of the passage. Once you have found number 3, number 4 is below it, never above.

A

We are living in an age that may be described as the age of the machine. Man's curiosity has led to the invention of thousands of machines. We today find ourselves surrounded by machines. We begin to make use of machines as soon as we wake up in the morning. We continue to take advantage of them till we go to sleep at night. Right from a fountain pen to an aircraft-all are manufactured by machinery.

B

Machines have, to a large extent, eliminated human labor. Machines are being employed in the agricultural processes. The sowing of seeds, the harvesting of crops, and many other agricultural activities are carried out with machines. Industry in the country is also mechanized. Our clothes and shoes are manufactured by machines. The tinned food, milk, butter, and so many other articles are processed and packed by machines.

C

Our means of communication, transport, and printing are dependent on machinery. The telegraph, the telephone, the wireless, the fax, and the IT system operate with machines. Cables are laid at the bottom of the seas with machines. The motorcar, the railway engine, the airplane, and the ship are all machines. Newspapers, magazines, and books are printed by machines that work at an amazing speed. In our households, we have several machines which we use daily. We have the cooking range, the washing machine, the refrigerator, the air-conditioner, etc. We wake to the alarm of a clock, we bathe with water heated by a machine; we eat food cooked with the help of machinery. In brief, machines have entered every field of life.

D

Long distances can be covered in no time. Machines have enabled man to land on the moon. They have made it possible for a man to explore space. Weather satellites and communication satellites are doing wonderful jobs. Machines have made man the master of the earth and the forces of nature.

E

Machines were made to serve man but they are rapidly becoming his masters. Man is becoming more and more dependent on machines. This supremacy of machines over man is a danger to mankind. Today man is entirely at the mercy of machines. They have dulled his sensibilities and blunted his mind and imagination. Man is fast losing his creative power. Not only this, machines threaten to destroy the world and mankind. Unfortunately, if the Nuclear War breaks out, mankind will be wiped out. Before the enormous powers of machines, man is a helpless creature. Since machines have come, they will stay.

A

Sportsmanship

449 words · paragraphs A–E · questions 41–48
A

Sportsmanship does not only mean playing games according to the rules of the game but it also means playing the game of life in accordance with the spirit learnt on the playing field. A true sportsman observes all these rules in life. A man who gives evidence of having a sporting spirit in the wider sphere of life but has not attained excellence in games is still a sportsman. Usually, those who play games develop into true sportsmen because their character is moulded by the training they are given on the field.

B

Sportsmanship means fair play. In a game, if a player plays foul, his side is penalised. Similarly, in the bigger game of life, one must be fair in one's dealings with others. Frankness, openness of heart, honesty and fairness are the qualities that a sportsman must have. One should not indulge in fraud or cheating if one wants to establish himself as a sportsman. He never hits anyone below the belt or exploits others.

C

Sportsmanship also stands for obedience to the leader. In a game, the players have to obey their captain. A team without order and obedience can never see the face of victory. They must have full confidence in the captain. In life also, a true sportsman yields to his superiors as he has respect for discipline in his heart and he knows that this is an essential feature of sportsmanship.

D

Team spirit is also an important component of sportsmanship. In a game, the various players co-operate with each other. Without co-operation success in a game for the team is impossible. In co-operation lies one's strength. Sportsmanship demands unity, cheerfulness even in the face of defeat. A loser in a game must accept his defeat without any grudge against the winner. This is the reason, a sportsman is generous to his enemies. He easily forgives those who have wronged him or been unjust to him and is never depressed by his setbacks. Failure never disheartens a sportsman, rather, it inspires him to greater efforts.

E

Recognition of the need for teamwork, fair play and respect for discipline are the dominant features of a sportsman. Sportsmanship is always worthy of admiration as it promotes human happiness. Life would be very attractive if all of us develop the spirit of sportsmanship. The pages of history reveal that the value of sportsmanship has always been appreciated and recognised. Sometimes it is found with regret that our sportsmen lack in the spirit of sportsmanship as they become victims to politics. But a true sportsman is he who plays the game with full dedication, without bothering of winning and losing. Victories and failures are to be taken in the same stride.
