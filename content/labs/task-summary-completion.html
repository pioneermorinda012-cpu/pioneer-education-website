Summary Completion

Nine Pioneer passages, 54 gaps to fill by typing — not clicking. This is the task where students lose marks to spelling and to the word limit rather than to comprehension, so the page tells you which of those went wrong: too many words, the right word misspelled, or simply the wrong word.

How to get these right

  1. The word must come from the passage. Your own word can mean exactly the right thing and still score zero. Copy what is written.
  2. Count the words before you write. NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS means two is the ceiling, not the target. Three words is wrong even when the meaning is perfect. A hyphenated word such as fair-price counts as one.
  3. Spelling is marked. The word is printed in front of you, so there is no excuse — copy it letter by letter. British and American spellings are both accepted.
  4. Read the whole summary first. It paraphrases the passage, so it will not share many words. Find which part of the passage it is retelling before you fill anything in.
  5. Decide what kind of word is missing. Look at the words on either side of the gap. After a or the you need a noun; after have you need a past form. This alone rules out most wrong answers.
  6. Keep the form the passage uses. If the passage says harvesting, do not write harvest. If it says joys, do not drop the s.
  7. The gaps follow the order of the passage. Once you have found gap 3, gap 4 is after it.
  8. Never leave a box empty. Nothing is deducted for a wrong guess.

A

Sportsmanship does not only mean playing games according to the rules of the game but it also means playing the game of life in accordance with the spirit learnt on the playing field. A true sportsman observes all these rules in life. A man who gives evidence of having a sporting spirit in the wider sphere of life but has not attained excellence in games is still a sportsman. Usually, those who play games develop into true sportsmen because their character is moulded by the training they are given on the field.

B

Sportsmanship means fair play. In a game, if a player plays foul, his side is penalised. Similarly, in the bigger game of life, one must be fair in one's dealings with others. Frankness, openness of heart, honesty and fairness are the qualities that a sportsman must have. One should not indulge in fraud or cheating if one wants to establish himself as a sportsman. He never hits anyone below the belt or exploits others.

C

Sportsmanship also stands for obedience to the leader. In a game, the players have to obey their captain. A team without order and obedience can never see the face of victory. They must have full confidence in the captain. In life also, a true sportsman yields to his superiors as he has respect for discipline in his heart and he knows that this is an essential feature of sportsmanship.

D

Team spirit is also an important component of sportsmanship. In a game, the various players co-operate with each other. Without co-operation success in a game for the team is impossible. In co-operation lies one's strength. Sportsmanship demands unity, cheerfulness even in the face of defeat. A loser in a game must accept his defeat without any grudge against the winner. This is the reason, a sportsman is generous to his enemies. He easily forgives those who have wronged him or been unjust to him and is never depressed by his setbacks. Failure never disheartens a sportsman, rather, it inspires him to greater efforts.

E

Recognition of the need for teamwork, fair play and respect for discipline are the dominant features of a sportsman. Sportsmanship is always worthy of admiration as it promotes human happiness. Life would be very attractive if all of us develop the spirit of sportsmanship. The pages of history reveal that the value of sportsmanship has always been appreciated and recognised. Sometimes it is found with regret that our sportsmen lack in the spirit of sportsmanship as they become victims to politics. But a true sportsman is he who plays the game with full dedication, without bothering of winning and losing. Victories and failures are to be taken in the same stride.

It means the desire to reduce all physical and mental distances to zero. It means the desire to advance ways and means of communication enough to make people live together as a global family Indeed over the last two decades the world has witnessed a great change. There has been a progressive transition from the traditional to the modern, from old to the new, and from national to global. These changes are manifested in almost all spheres-social, economic, political, cultural, and ethical. In short, as seen in the East, this change in society is largely due to the influence of the western world.

B

The world, today, has become unipolar and globalization is supposed to have brought numerous benefits to the developing nations. Globalization, it is believed, has reduced the yawning disparities among industrialized countries and third-world countries. This has been done by acceptance by the developing countries the models of economic management of the advanced countries.

C

But globalization has not improved the world economic situation. It has only increased the economic disparities between the nations. This is seen in the disparities in consumption in the globalized world, the rich people spend eighty percent on private consumption while the poor spend only four percent. The gap between the rich and the poor is growing at a great pace. The western countries, acting through the IMF and World Trade Organization have mismanaged the processes of privatization, liberalization, and stabilization. Globalization is not working for the stability of the world economy, it is not working for many of the world's poor because liberalized economic policies and decentralization processes which are unleashed as a result of globalization are not backed up with adequate social security measures.

D

Besides, globalization has harmed many spheres. It has brought consumerism and materialism and the degradation of the environment. It has led to the rapid erosion of values and general cynicism about institutions. Another unfortunate development is the alienation of the younger generation as expressed in their rejection of tradition and loss of faith in social political and cultural traditions. It has also led to the spread of international terrorism.

E

If the present state of rising inequalities, social tensions, and psychological dysfunction is to be improved, a new world order must be discovered. Such a world can only be discovered by understanding man's relation to his worlds, man's relation to the ecosystem. We must create a society that has a reasonable standard of living with peace and harmony. To be free from consumerism and materialism, we should aim at a globalized economy in which weaker sections of society and their interests are sufficiently protected by adequate social security measures.

The Problem of Rising Prices

480 words · paragraphs A–G · questions 49–54
A

Rising prices are one of the formidable problems that India is facing today. It has made life unbearable. It has broken the back of common people. Despite working hard they find it hard to make both their ends meet. There is widespread discontent among them. There are protest rallies and angry demonstrations but there is no relief. The number of people below the poverty line is increasing day by day. The distressed people often say that the British rule was better than the present rule.

B

The worst hit in this matter is the middle class. The condition of the middle-class people has worsened. Most of the people of this class are salaried men. They get a fixed pay. But prices have risen higher and higher. Taxes have increased many times. It has become difficult for middle-class people to live up to some standard. The prices of grains, pulses, and clothes have shot up. The middle class is on the verge of starvation.

C

One of the major factors for the rise in prices is the faulty policies of the Government of India. There are inflationary trends in the Indian economy. There is deficit financing in the provisions of the budget each year. There is no check on the government expenditure. The purchasing power of money has come down. Consumerism is on the increase.

D

Price-line is linked with the demand and supply principle. With the uncontrolled growth of the population demand is increasing. But supply can't keep pace with it. With the explosion of the population at our door, any talk of controlling the demand for essentials of life is like putting the cart before the horse Policymakers and planners know fully well the harm that an uncontrolled population can cause to the system All efforts to check the growth of population have proved futile.

E

Black money is also responsible for an unchecked rise in prices. Black money which has been generated over the years has increased the rich-poor gap. There is a ‘parallel economy’ working in the country. It has distorted all talk of 'socio-economic equality’ and rule of law. With bags full of 'black money and 'five-star culture, all talk of controlling price-rise sounds ridiculous.

F

Artificial scarcities created by 'bandhs, strikes, curfews' also hike prices. Agitations and protests in the area of supply disturb the supply line. Black marketeers and hoarders take undue advantage of this situation. They hoard goods and sell them at higher than normal rates. Experience shows that once the prices go up, they refuse to come down even if the situation has improved.

G

The government should pay serious attention to check rising prices. It should remodel its economic policies and make them result-oriented. The growth of population and generation of black money should be curbed. Articles of daily use should be made available at fair-price shops Those who create artificial scarcities should be firmly dealt with.
