🏆 31 Speaking Formulas for IELTS

Original Pioneer Education method · 31 ready-to-use answer patterns for Parts 1, 2 and 3

1A-R-E: Answer, Reason, ExamplePart 1

Use it for: Almost any Part 1 question. Your default safety net.

The formula

  1. Answer directly in the first sentence
  2. Reason: give one “because…”
  3. Example: add one real, specific detail

Model

Q: Do you enjoy cooking?

Yes, quite a lot. It helps me switch off after a long day of teaching. Last Sunday, for instance, I spent two hours making rajma from scratch and my whole family finished it.

Power phrases

Yes, quite a lot.The main reason is…For instance, / Last week…

Avoid

⚠️ Stopping after “Yes, I do.” — a one-word answer scores low on fluency.

Practise

Do you like your hometown? Answer in A-R-E.

2The Three-Sentence StretchPart 1

Use it for: When you freeze and need length fast.

The formula

  1. Sentence 1: direct answer
  2. Sentence 2: expand with a reason or feeling
  3. Sentence 3: a detail, comparison or result

Model

Q: What kind of music do you like?

I mostly listen to Punjabi folk. It reminds me of village weddings and gives me a lot of energy. Compared with pop, I find the lyrics more meaningful.

Power phrases

Mostly…It reminds me of…Compared with…, I find…

Avoid

⚠️ Giving four or five sentences on easy questions and running out of ideas for hard ones. Aim for two to four.

Practise

Do you prefer mornings or evenings? Use three sentences.

3Past–Present–Future SwingPart 1

Use it for: “Did you…”, “Have you always…”, “Would you like to…” type questions.

The formula

  1. Past: how it was
  2. Present: how it is now
  3. Future: what you hope or expect

Model

Q: Do you enjoy reading?

I didn’t read much as a child, only school books. These days I read for about half an hour before bed, mainly biographies. I’d love to finish at least twenty books next year.

Power phrases

As a child, I…These days, I…In the future, I’d like to…

Avoid

⚠️ Using one tense for the whole answer.

Practise

Describe your relationship with technology: past, present, future.

4Prefer-Over FormulaPart 1

Use it for: “Which do you prefer…” and “Do you prefer… or…” questions.

The formula

  1. Pick one side clearly
  2. Say why it suits you
  3. Give a quick contrast with the other side

Model

Q: Do you prefer eating at home or in restaurants?

I’d rather eat at home. Home food is healthier and I know exactly what goes into it. Restaurants are fun for special occasions, but I wouldn’t want to go out every week.

Power phrases

I’d rather… than…I tend to prefer… because……is fun, but…

Avoid

⚠️ Saying “both” with no explanation. If you choose both, say when you choose each.

Practise

Do you prefer the city or the countryside?

5Frequency + Habit StoryPart 1

Use it for: “How often…” and “When do you usually…” questions.

The formula

  1. Give the frequency with a varied phrase
  2. Say the typical time or place
  3. Add one habit or routine detail

Model

Q: How often do you go shopping?

Not very often, maybe once a fortnight. I usually go on Saturday afternoons when the market is less crowded, and I always carry a list so I don’t overspend.

Power phrases

Maybe once a fortnightOn the whole, I… every…It depends on…, but usually…

Avoid

⚠️ Repeating “sometimes” and “always” again and again; vary frequency expressions.

Practise

How often do you use social media?

6Used-To Contrast (Then vs Now)Part 1

Use it for: Childhood, change and “has it changed” questions.

The formula

  1. Then: “used to / would”
  2. Now: present simple
  3. Reason for the change

Model

Q: Has your hometown changed much?

Quite a bit. It used to be a small, quiet town with only a few shops, but now there are malls and coaching centres everywhere. I think the growth in population is the main reason.

Power phrases

used to…, but now…It has become much more…The main reason is…

Avoid

⚠️ Using “used to” for a state that is still true; use it only for finished past habits.

Practise

How have schools changed since you were a child?

7The Hypothetical RescuePart 1

Use it for: Odd or unfamiliar questions where you have no real experience.

The formula

  1. Admit it lightly: “I’ve never really…”
  2. Switch to “If I had to… I would…”
  3. Give a reasoned guess

Model

Q: Would you like to go into space?

I’ve never really thought about it. If I were offered a seat, I’d probably say yes, because the view of Earth must be breathtaking, although the training would worry me.

Power phrases

I’ve never really thought about it, but…If I had to choose, I’d…I imagine that…

Avoid

⚠️ Saying “I don’t know” and stopping. Always continue with an imagined answer.

Practise

Would you like to be famous?

8Yes–But Partial AnswerPart 1

Use it for: Questions that ask “Do you think…?” where the truth is mixed.

The formula

  1. Yes / No, with a limit: “mostly”, “to some extent”
  2. But + exception
  3. Balanced closing thought

Model

Q: Do you think everyone should learn a musical instrument?

To some extent, yes. It builds discipline and patience, but not everyone has the talent or the time, so it shouldn’t be compulsory.

Power phrases

To some extent…Generally speaking, …, but…That said, …

Avoid

⚠️ Extreme answers (“always”, “never”) that you can’t support.

Practise

Is it important to dress well for work?

9The 60-Second Planning GridPart 2

Use it for: The minute you get to prepare for the cue card.

The formula

  1. Write the topic in the centre
  2. Add 5W: Who, What, Where, When, Why
  3. Add 1 feeling + 1 power word
  4. Number the order you will speak

Model

Q: Describe a book you enjoyed reading.

Notes: Book = “Wings of Fire” · who recommended = teacher · when = Class 10 · why = inspired me · feeling = motivated · power words: gripping, resilience.

Power phrases

I’d like to talk about…The reason I picked this is…

Avoid

⚠️ Writing full sentences in the minute; use keywords only.

Practise

Plan “a place you like to visit” in 60 seconds.

10The Opening HookPart 2

Use it for: The first 10 seconds of your long turn.

The formula

  1. Name the topic in a fresh way
  2. Add a short hook (surprise, feeling or contrast)
  3. Preview what you will say

Model

Q: Describe a teacher who influenced you.

I’d like to talk about Mr Sharma, my maths teacher, who changed the way I think about failure. I’ll explain who he is, how we met, and what I learned.

Power phrases

I’d like to talk about…Where do I start?Without a doubt, … is someone who…

Avoid

⚠️ Starting with “Today I will talk about…” every time.

Practise

Open a talk about a festival.

11The Story SpinePart 2

Use it for: Event, experience or memory cue cards.

The formula

  1. Before: set the scene
  2. Then: the main event
  3. After: the result and your reaction

Model

Q: Describe a time you helped someone.

It was last monsoon. A man’s scooter had broken down near my house in heavy rain. I let him wait under our porch and called a mechanic. Two hours later he was on his way and later brought sweets to thank us. It reminded me that small help matters.

Power phrases

It all began when…Out of the blue…In the end…Looking back…

Avoid

⚠️ Telling the story in the wrong order.

Practise

Describe a memorable journey with the story spine.

12PEOPLE: Person DescriptionPart 2

Use it for: “Describe a person…” cue cards.

The formula

  1. P — Position: who they are to you
  2. E — Earlier: how you met
  3. O — Outstanding trait
  4. P — Pastimes or things you do together
  5. L — Lessons learned
  6. E — Emotion: how you feel about them

Model

Q: Describe a friend you admire.

Ravi is my closest friend and also my former classmate. We met in Class 6 on the first day of school. He’s the most hard-working person I know. On weekends we play cricket together, and he has taught me to stay calm under pressure. I feel lucky to know him.

Power phrases

He/She is someone who…What stands out is…We have a lot in common.

Avoid

⚠️ Listing only age, job and appearance.

Practise

Describe a neighbour using PEOPLE.

13SPACE: Place DescriptionPart 2

Use it for: “Describe a place…” cue cards.

The formula

  1. S — Scene: where it is and what it looks like
  2. P — People you see there
  3. A — Activities done there
  4. C — Climate / atmosphere
  5. E — Experience and your feelings

Model

Q: Describe a place you like to visit.

The Sirhind canal is about ten kilometres from my town. It is a wide, peaceful stretch with trees on both sides. Families cycle and picnic there at weekends. The breeze is cool in the evening. I go when I feel stressed because it clears my head.

Power phrases

It’s located…The atmosphere is…There’s a real sense of…

Avoid

⚠️ Describing only the name and the location.

Practise

Describe a market using SPACE.

14Object Profile: LOOK-USE-STORYPart 2

Use it for: “Describe something you own / a gadget / a gift…” cue cards.

The formula

  1. L — Look: size, colour, material
  2. U — Use: what you do with it
  3. S — Story: how you got it
  4. Y — Your feelings about it

Model

Q: Describe something you own that is important to you.

It’s a small silver watch with a brown leather strap. I wear it to every class because I like to be punctual. My father gave it to me when I got my first job. It’s not expensive, but it’s priceless to me.

Power phrases

It’s made of…I use it to…It holds sentimental value.

Avoid

⚠️ Forgetting to say why it matters.

Practise

Describe a gadget with LOOK-USE-STORY.

15Event Frame: BEDAPart 2

Use it for: “Describe an event / celebration / occasion…” cue cards.

The formula

  1. B — Background: when and why
  2. E — Events: what happened in order
  3. D — Details: sights, sounds, people
  4. A — Afterthought: how it ended and what you felt

Model

Q: Describe a wedding you attended.

It was my cousin’s wedding two years ago, held in a village near Ropar. The baraat arrived with dhol and dancing, the food was wonderful, and everyone stayed until midnight. Afterwards I felt emotional because the whole family was together.

Power phrases

It took place…The highlight was…What I’ll never forget is…

Avoid

⚠️ Only listing events without detail.

Practise

Describe a festival with BEDA.

16The Lesson LandingPart 2

Use it for: Experience or challenge cards that ask “what did you learn?”.

The formula

  1. State the challenge
  2. Say the action you took
  3. Land on a clear lesson

Model

Q: Describe a difficult task you completed.

Preparing for my driving test was hard because I was nervous on busy roads. I practised daily at six in the morning when the roads were empty. The lesson was that fear shrinks when you prepare.

Power phrases

The lesson I took away is…It taught me that…If I could do it again, I’d…

Avoid

⚠️ Ending abruptly after the last event.

Practise

Describe a skill you learned and the lesson it taught.

17Sensory LayeringPart 2

Use it for: Make any description vivid and win lexical-resource points.

The formula

  1. Sight: colours, shapes
  2. Sound
  3. Smell / taste
  4. Touch / temperature
  5. Feeling

Model

Q: Describe a favourite food.

Parathas straight from the tawa smell of ghee and warmth. They’re crisp on the outside and soft inside, and there’s a gentle sizzle as the butter melts. Just thinking of it makes me feel at home.

Power phrases

It smells like…It has a… textureIt gives off a… feeling

Avoid

⚠️ Using only “nice”, “good” and “beautiful”.

Practise

Describe a rainy day with three senses.

18The Expansion Ladder (Fill 2 Minutes)Part 2

Use it for: When you run out of ideas at 1 minute.

The formula

  1. What?
  2. Why?
  3. How?
  4. When?
  5. Who else?
  6. What if?

Model

Q: Describe a hobby you enjoy.

(You have covered what and why.) How I started: a friend lent me a guitar. When I practise: every night for 20 minutes. Who else: my brother joins in. What if I stopped: I’d feel something was missing.

Power phrases

Another thing worth mentioning is…Apart from that…To add to that…

Avoid

⚠️ Ending at one minute. Always have three more ladder rungs ready.

Practise

Take any cue card and extend it with the ladder.

19The Closing LinePart 2

Use it for: The last 10 seconds of Part 2.

The formula

  1. Signal that you are finishing
  2. Repeat the main feeling in new words
  3. Stop smoothly

Model

Q: (Any cue card)

So, that’s my favourite teacher. Overall, he made me believe in myself and I’ll always be grateful.

Power phrases

So, in a nutshell…All in all…That’s pretty much it.

Avoid

⚠️ Fading out with “That’s all, thank you” or “I don’t know what else to say”.

Practise

Write three closing lines for any topic.

20The Rounding-Off QuestionPart 2

Use it for: The examiner asks one short follow-up after Part 2.

The formula

  1. Answer in one or two sentences
  2. Use a direct answer + brief reason

Model

Q: Would you recommend this book to others?

Definitely, especially to teenagers, because it shows how determination can change your life.

Power phrases

Definitely, because…Not really, since…

Avoid

⚠️ Giving a long speech; this question is short.

Practise

Prepare a follow-up for your last cue card.

21PEEL ParagraphPart 3

Use it for: The standard formula for Part 3 opinions.

The formula

  1. P — Point: your answer
  2. E — Explain the reason
  3. E — Example
  4. L — Link back to the question

Model

Q: Why do people travel abroad?

People mainly travel to experience different cultures. Meeting people with other lifestyles broadens your outlook. For example, a friend of mine who visited Japan came back much more open-minded. So travel is really a form of education.

Power phrases

The main reason is…To illustrate…That’s why…

Avoid

⚠️ Giving only the point with no support.

Practise

Why do people watch sport?

22Both-Sides BalancePart 3

Use it for: “Is it good or bad?”, “Do advantages outweigh disadvantages?”

The formula

  1. Start with the main benefit
  2. Add the downside with “however”
  3. Give your overall view

Model

Q: Is social media good for young people?

On the one hand, it helps them stay in touch and learn new things. On the other hand, it can waste time and affect mental health. Overall, I think the benefits are real provided that usage is limited.

Power phrases

On the one hand… on the other hand…While it’s true that…All things considered…

Avoid

⚠️ Sitting on the fence forever; end with a view.

Practise

Are online classes better than classroom classes?

23Past vs Present ComparisonPart 3

Use it for: “How has … changed?” questions.

The formula

  1. In the past…
  2. Nowadays…
  3. The main reason for the change
  4. Evaluation: better or worse?

Model

Q: How has shopping changed?

In the past, people went to local markets and bargained face to face. Nowadays many buy online with a few taps. This is mainly due to smartphones and cheap internet. It is more convenient, though it has hurt small shopkeepers.

Power phrases

Back in the day,…Whereas today…This shift is largely due to…

Avoid

⚠️ Describing only the present.

Practise

How has communication changed?

24Cause → Effect → SolutionPart 3

Use it for: Problems: pollution, traffic, stress, unemployment.

The formula

  1. Name the cause
  2. Describe the effect
  3. Offer a practical solution

Model

Q: Why is traffic a problem in cities?

There are too many private vehicles because public transport is limited. As a result, roads are jammed and the air is polluted. Governments could improve buses and metro lines and charge fees to enter busy areas.

Power phrases

One major cause is…This leads to…One possible solution would be…

Avoid

⚠️ Complaining about the problem with no solution.

Practise

How can we reduce plastic waste?

25If-Would HypothesisPart 3

Use it for: “What would happen if…” and “Should the government…”

The formula

  1. If + past simple
  2. would + base verb
  3. Add a condition or consequence

Model

Q: What would happen if everyone worked from home?

If everyone worked from home, cities would be less crowded and commuting costs would fall. However, some people would feel isolated and teamwork might suffer.

Power phrases

If that were the case,…I imagine that…It would probably…

Avoid

⚠️ Using “will” instead of “would”.

Practise

What if there were no cars?

26Some-Others ContrastPart 3

Use it for: Debate-style questions such as “Some people think… do you agree?”

The formula

  1. Some people believe…
  2. Others argue…
  3. Personally, I lean towards…

Model

Q: Should university education be free?

Some people argue that it should be free so everyone has equal opportunity. Others say that this would be too costly for governments. Personally, I believe it should be subsidised for needy students rather than totally free.

Power phrases

Some people argue that…Others point out that…I’m inclined to think…

Avoid

⚠️ Presenting only one side.

Practise

Should children use phones at school?

27The Future ForecastPart 3

Use it for: “Will it change in the future?”, “What do you think will happen?”

The formula

  1. Prediction with a hedge
  2. Reason or trend
  3. Possible impact

Model

Q: Will books disappear in the future?

I doubt printed books will disappear completely, but digital reading will probably grow. More people already read on phones, so publishers will adapt, although many readers will keep their love of paper.

Power phrases

I would expect…It’s likely that…There’s a good chance that…

Avoid

⚠️ Predicting extremes with certainty.

Practise

Will cash disappear?

28Safe GeneralisingPart 3

Use it for: Opinions about “people”, “young people”, “men and women”.

The formula

  1. Use softeners: tend to, in many cases
  2. Add an exception
  3. Avoid stereotypes

Model

Q: Do young people spend too much time online?

Many young people tend to spend several hours online, but this isn’t true of everyone. In many cases they use it to study or to socialise.

Power phrases

tend toby and largein many casesnot necessarily

Avoid

⚠️ Using “all”, “everyone”, “nobody”.

Practise

Are older people less adaptable?

29Thinking-Time PhrasesAll parts

Use it for: Any question that needs a second to think.

The formula

  1. Use a natural phrase
  2. Repeat part of the question
  3. Then begin your answer

Model

Q: What is the best age to get married?

That’s an interesting question. Let me think… I’d say somewhere between twenty-five and thirty, because by then people are usually financially stable.

Power phrases

That’s an interesting question.Let me think…Well, I’d say…Good question, actually.

Avoid

⚠️ Silence for too long, or endless “umm”.

Practise

Practise three different fillers.

30Paraphrase & Clarify RescueAll parts

Use it for: Unknown word, forgotten word or unclear question.

The formula

  1. Ask politely or describe the word
  2. Use “a kind of…”, “something you use to…”
  3. Continue without stopping

Model

Q: (You forget the word “umbrella”)

It’s that thing you hold over your head when it rains.

Power phrases

Could you repeat that, please?What I mean is…It’s a kind of…

Avoid

⚠️ Switching to your native language or freezing.

Practise

Describe five objects without naming them.

31Self-Repair & Clean FinishAll parts

Use it for: When you make a slip, or need to correct yourself.

The formula

  1. Pause
  2. Correct calmly
  3. Continue the sentence

Model

Q: (You say “He go to work”)

He go— sorry, he goes to work by bus every day.

Power phrases

Sorry, I mean…Let me rephrase that.What I meant to say is…

Avoid

⚠️ Panicking or repeating the same error.

Practise

Record yourself and fix three slips.

Use each formula as a skeleton, never as a script. Fill it with your own true ideas — examiners reward natural, personal answers.
